What changes on track

It isn't really about pace. Set the difficulty you like and they'll still run it. What changes is how they race each other.

Stock AI

  • Follows a slower car around for laps instead of trying anything
  • Doesn't cover the inside, so getting past costs you nothing
  • Laps like a metronome from green to checkered
  • One spin and that car's day is over, right where it stopped
  • Turn one is either a pileup or nothing at all

With Verve

  • Closes up, leans on you, takes the inside if you leave it open
  • Gets one clean move to defend, then has to race it out
  • Drivers drift lap to lap, go off late in a stint, get twitchy under pressure
  • Spun cars can get themselves going and wait for a gap to rejoin
  • The field holds its grid side into turn one

It's free and I'm not planning a paid version. If you feel like throwing a few bucks at it, there's a Ko-fi.

Under the hood

Six things going on in the background while you race.

Racecraft 01

Cars close up and lean on whoever's ahead, dive the inside when it's open, switch back when it isn't, and try the side the defender isn't covering. Defending is one move, then they have to race it out. Collision awareness stays on, so mostly they place the car instead of punting it.

Tactics change with the class. An F1 car builds a run from a long way back and picks its spot; a touring car just sends it up the inside.

Human pace 02

Every driver gets a bit of a personality: a baseline pace, some lap-to-lap drift, tyres going off at the end of a stint, nerves when someone's filling their mirrors, a tow down the straight.

The field spreads out the way a real one does instead of showing up in a neat queue.

Class-aware physics 03

Cold tyres need heat, and slicks need a lot more of it than old bias-ply rubber. Rain makes them careful, more so the wetter it gets. High-downforce cars lose front grip in dirty air through a corner and get it back on the straight, so they'll close right up and then have to work for the pass.

The class is read off the car itself: its tags first, then its own physics files. If a mod throws that off, you can set it yourself.

Honest mistakes 04

Small bobbles now and then on cars that'll forgive one, and none at all on high-downforce open-wheelers, since those don't really wobble in real life either. Grip changes are smoothed out, so a mistake costs a bit of time rather than pitching the car into a wall.

Self-recovery 05

A car that's spun or beached but not actually wrecked can get itself going again: gentle throttle, steering back toward the line, reverse out of a wall or out of a two-car lock, and a wait for traffic before it rejoins.

It stays out of the race start and the pit exit, and genuinely wrecked cars still retire.

A difficulty slider that means it 06

80 is a beginner field, 90 club level, 100 quick, and whatever you pick is what you get on every car. It works in Career too, which stock AC just ignored.

Rather build a grid by hand? Any slot can have its own driver, with its own pace, aggression and appetite for risk.

New · companion app · beta

Now there's a race engineer

Spotter apps read your telemetry and narrate all of it, so you stop listening. Verve Engineer does the opposite: it stays quiet while you're busy in a corner or chasing a lap, and speaks up when something actually changes.

Knows when to talk 01

Every call it could make gets scored and rate-limited, and held back while you're mid-corner or on a hot lap. It only speaks when the call earns its place, in a voice that sounds like a person on a radio rather than a dashboard being read out.

Reads the race 02

Lap times and deltas, flags, fuel and tyre projections, your pit window, track limits, laps to go. Ask it out loud or on a hotkey: how are my tyres, what's the gap, box this lap, keep it quiet.

Spots for you 03

Its bundled in-game app calls cars left and right, still there or clear, and reads the gaps front and back with how fast they're closing. No CSP needed for that part.

Sharper with Verve 04

With Verve running it reads what the AI is about to do (defending the inside here, lunge coming, hold your line), plus yellows ahead, rivals pitting and the fastest lap of the race.

Two modes on one toggle: standard keeps it to what matters, chatty adds the spotter and running commentary. Four engineer voices ship inside the app, so there's no account or key to set up. It's a separate download that runs on its own with stock Assetto Corsa and gets smarter when Verve is alongside it.

Three steps

A minute, give or take.

Grab the zip

From OverTake, or the latest GitHub release if you'd rather. It's a couple hundred KB.

Drop it on Content Manager

Drag the zip onto CM and confirm. No need to unzip it. Doing it by hand instead? Unzip so the folder ends up at assettocorsa/apps/lua/Verve.

Open Verve in the sidebar

Start a race, open Verve AI from the in-game sidebar, check Enable Verve. That's the setup. Everything else in that window is optional.

Couple of things worth knowing: keep damage at 50% or more, and turn off any other AI mod. Verve will try to warn you in its own window if it spots one, but two apps writing the same AI values mostly just end up fighting each other.
