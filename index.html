What actually changes

None of this is the difficulty slider in disguise. The cars still run the pace you asked for — they just race each other the way people do.

Stock AI

  • Sits behind a slower car for laps rather than trying a move
  • Never covers a line, so a pass costs you nothing
  • Every driver laps like a metronome, start to finish
  • One spin ends that car's day — it stays where it stopped
  • Turn one is either a pile-up or nothing at all

With Verve

  • Closes up, pressures, and takes the inside when it opens
  • Makes one clean defensive move, then holds the line
  • Drivers drift lap to lap, fade late in a stint, feel pressure
  • Spun cars pick themselves up and rejoin when it is safe
  • The field holds its grid side all the way to the first apex

It is free, and there is no paid version coming. If you would rather throw a few quid at it than nothing, there is a Ko-fi.

Under the hood

Six things running every frame, for every AI car on track.

Racecraft 01

Cars close up and apply pressure, dive the inside of a corner when it is open, switch back when it is not, and pass on the side the defender is not covering. A defender gets one move, then it has to race. Collision awareness stays on, so they place a car rather than punt one.

Tactics go by class: a formula car builds the run from a long way back and places the pass precisely, a touring car leans on the brakes and dives.

Human pace 02

Every driver gets a little personality of their own — a baseline pace, lap-to-lap drift, tyre fade at the end of a stint, nerves under pressure from behind, and a tow down the straights.

The field strings out the way a real one does instead of arriving in a neat queue.

Class-aware physics 03

Cold tyres need heat, and slicks need far more of it than vintage rubber. Rain makes drivers careful in proportion to how wet it is. High-downforce cars lose front grip in dirty air through a corner and get it back on the straight, so they close up and then have to work.

The class is read off the car itself — its tags, then its own physics files — and you can override it when a mod fools the guess.

Honest mistakes 04

Occasional small bobbles on forgiving cars, and none at all on high-downforce open-wheelers, which do not wobble in real life either. Grip changes are rate-limited, so a mistake costs a driver time instead of snapping the car into a barrier.

Self-recovery 05

A spun or beached car that is not actually wrecked gets itself going again: gentle throttle, steering back towards the line, reverse out of a wall or out of a two-car lock, and a wait for traffic before it rejoins.

It never touches the race start or the pit exit, and genuinely wrecked cars still retire.

A difficulty slider that means it 06

80 is a beginner field, 90 club level, 100 a quick one — and the number you pick is the number you get, on every car. It works in Career too, where the game used to ignore it entirely.

Want to build a grid by hand instead? Give any slot its own driver, with its own pace, aggression and appetite for risk.

Three steps

About a minute, start to finish.

Get the zip

From OverTake, or the latest GitHub release if you prefer. It is a couple of hundred kilobytes.

Drop it on Content Manager

Drag the zip onto CM and confirm the install. No need to unzip it. Doing it by hand instead? Unzip so the folder lands at assettocorsa/apps/lua/Verve.

Open Verve in the sidebar

Start any race, open Verve AI from the in-game sidebar, tick Enable Verve. That is the whole setup — everything else in that window is optional.

Two things worth knowing: keep damage at 50% or more, and switch off any other AI mod. Verve will say so in its own window if it spots one — two apps writing the same AI values just fight each other.
